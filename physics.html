Electric Charge

Charge is a fundamental physical property of matter responsible for electrical interaction. This topic establishes the language used throughout electrostatics.

Core concept

What is electric charge?

Electric charge is a fundamental property of matter because of which bodies experience and produce electrical interactions. At the microscopic level, atoms contain negatively charged electrons, positively charged protons, and electrically neutral neutrons.

+q
Conceptual representation of a positive charge

Scope note: Electrostatics studies charges at rest. The next major topic, Current Electricity, studies the effects of charges in organized motion.

Sign and magnitude

Elementary charge

The magnitude of the elementary charge is

\[e = 1.602\,176\,634\times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}\]

For the JEE level, it is commonly approximated as \(e\approx 1.6\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}\).

Electron\(q_e=-e\)
Proton\(q_p=+e\)
Neutron\(q_n=0\)

Remember: The sign tells the nature of the charge relative to the chosen convention; the magnitude tells how much charge is present.

Property 01

Additivity of charge

Charge is an algebraic scalar quantity. The net charge of a system is the algebraic sum of the charges it contains:

\[Q_{\mathrm{net}}=q_1+q_2+q_3+\cdots=\sum_i q_i\]

Always include signs while adding. For example, \(+5\,\mathrm{C}\) and \(-2\,\mathrm{C}\) give \(Q_{\mathrm{net}}=+3\,\mathrm{C}\), not \(7\,\mathrm{C}\).

Property 02

Conservation of charge

In an isolated system, the total electric charge remains constant. Charge is neither created nor destroyed; it is transferred from one body to another.

\[Q_{\mathrm{initial}}=Q_{\mathrm{final}}\]

If a neutral body loses three electrons, it becomes positively charged by \(+3e\):

\[\Delta Q=+3e\approx+4.8\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}\]

The receiving body gains the same amount and becomes negatively charged by \(-3e\), provided the two-body system is isolated.

Property 03

Quantisation of charge

For ordinary observable bodies, the net charge occurs in integral multiples of the elementary charge:

\[q=ne,\qquad n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\ldots\]
  • For an electron deficit, \(n>0\) and the body is positively charged.
  • For an excess of electrons, \(n<0\) and the body is negatively charged.
  • A value such as \(2.5e\) is not allowed for the net charge of an ordinary isolated body.

JEE convention: Use \(q=ne\) with integer \(n\). Quark fractional charges belong to particle physics and do not change the standard Class 12/JEE treatment of observable net charge.

Important distinction

Charge, mass and weight are different ideas

An object containing charge necessarily contains matter and therefore has mass, but the presence of mass does not imply that the object has a net charge. A neutral object can have enormous mass.

Mass

Mass is an intrinsic property of an object. In elementary mechanics, it is not the same as weight and does not change merely because the object is moved to another planet.

Weight

Weight is gravitational force, \(W=mg\), so it changes when the local gravitational acceleration \(g\) changes.

Charge

Net charge is measured in coulombs and remains the same when the object is moved between locations, unless charge is transferred to or from it.

Neutral body

Neutral means net charge is zero; it does not mean that the body contains no charged particles.

JEE layer

JEE Main

Remember \(e\), the sign convention, \(q=ne\), charge transfer by electron movement, and algebraic addition.

JEE Advanced

Track the complete isolated system, distinguish electron transfer from proton transfer, and use conservation before calculating individual charges.

Common traps

“Loses electrons” means positive charge; “gains electrons” means negative charge. Do not use the unsigned magnitude when the question asks for signed charge.

Quick recall

A-C-Q: Additive, Conserved, Quantised. Sign comes from electron deficit or excess.

Coulomb’s Law

Coulomb’s law gives the electrostatic force between two point charges. It is the starting point for analysing electric interaction quantitatively.

Core law

Magnitude of electrostatic force

For two point charges with magnitudes \(|q_1|\) and \(|q_2|\), separated by distance \(r\) in vacuum or air, the magnitude of the electrostatic force is

\[F=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}=k\frac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}\]

The proportionality statement is \(F\propto |q_1q_2|\) and \(F\propto 1/r^2\). The absolute value is used when writing the magnitude, because magnitude cannot be negative.

SymbolMeaningSI unit / value
\(F\)Magnitude of electrostatic forcenewton (\(\mathrm{N}\))
\(q_1,q_2\)Charges of the two point particlescoulomb (\(\mathrm{C}\))
\(r\)Distance between their centresmetre (\(\mathrm{m}\))
\(\varepsilon_0\)Permittivity of free space\(8.854\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{C^2N^{-1}m^{-2}}\)
\(k\)Coulomb constant in vacuum\(\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\approx9.0\times10^9\ \mathrm{Nm^2C^{-2}}\)
Direction and sign

Like repel; unlike attract

The force acts along the line joining the two charges. The sign of \(q_1q_2\) determines the nature of the interaction:

Like charges

For \(q_1q_2>0\), two positive or two negative charges repel each other.

Unlike charges

For \(q_1q_2<0\), a positive and a negative charge attract each other.

\[\vec F_{12}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\,\hat{\mathbf r}_{12}\]

In the vector form, direction is carried by the unit vector and the signed product. In the magnitude form, use \(|q_1q_2|\) and describe attraction or repulsion separately.

Newton’s third law: The forces on the two charges are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction: \(\vec F_{12}=-\vec F_{21}\).

Conditions and JEE layer

When can we use Coulomb’s law directly?

  • Use it directly for ideal point charges or spherically symmetric charge distributions treated as point charges from outside.
  • For finite bodies, the separation must be large compared with their dimensions, or the geometry must justify the point-charge approximation.
  • In a material medium, replace \(\varepsilon_0\) by \(\varepsilon=K\varepsilon_0\), so \(F=\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon}\dfrac{|q_1q_2|}{r^2}=\dfrac{F_0}{K}\).
  • For multiple charges, calculate each pairwise force and add them vectorially using superposition.

JEE Main

Know the formula, units, direction, sign-based attraction/repulsion, and quick ratios such as \(F_2/F_1=(r_1/r_2)^2\).

JEE Advanced

Use vectors, symmetry, superposition and medium dependence. Never add force magnitudes when directions differ.

Common traps

Do not use signed \(q_1q_2\) inside a magnitude formula; do not forget square on distance; do not use distance from an arbitrary origin instead of separation between charges.

Fast ratio

If charge product changes by a factor \(a\) and distance changes by a factor \(b\), then \(F\) changes by \(a/b^2\).
